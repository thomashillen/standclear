# Train motion on Apple Maps

A moving car shows an estimated position along its reported subway segment. A usable vehicle timestamp, matching trip, route, direction, next stop and future ETA are required for interpolation. Trains stop at the predicted platform. Without a matching ETA, observations retain the existing discrete estimate except for the bounded reconciliation described below. Missing or failed feeds and expired snapshots use the live map’s existing visibility rules.

The engine indexes track distances when static data changes, keeps one trajectory per current trip, and samples along the polyline. Small compatible ETA corrections retain their existing blend. A changed position can also reconcile along supported track over four seconds, starting at the pose actually being shown, including an interrupted transition. This covers eligible stopped-at station advances without an ETA, larger corrections up to 2 km, and corrected payloads with the same generation timestamp. It does not invent onward travel or a new arrival prediction.

Reconciliation requires the same trip, reported service and known north/south direction, explicit fresh vehicle timestamps on both observations, supported statuses, and same, linked or adjacent platform geometry. Both the current target and the four-second predicted endpoint must be within 2 km. Discrete platform positions offset more than 0.5 metres from the representative track stay discrete rather than snapping to the shape. Invalid geometry, unknown timestamps/direction, unrelated identities, distant/unlinked stops and stale evidence do not qualify. Repeated identical inputs preserve an existing trajectory instead of restarting it.

During reconciliation a dashed outline and **Estimated position** caption distinguish the uncertain pose; VoiceOver says **Current position is uncertain**. This describes presentation uncertainty, not exact live tracking. The vehicle's existing 90-second movement cap can freeze a transition partway; its caption remains truthful and the separate stale-report announcement remains. The snapshot still expires at 60 seconds. Failed feeds and lifecycle cleanup discard the trajectory. Camera pan, heading, zoom and the selected route are independent of these updates.

Only the visible, active Apple map samples moving cars, at most four times per second. With Reduce Motion or no eligible movement the map samples discretely for age and expiry. Leaving the map or resigning the active scene clears motion history. A fresh cached observation can appear discretely on reentry until the next generation. The web client retains its own rendering behavior.

Dense-map pan and zoom, battery use and fold transitions still need device measurements. Different reported trains may be eligible to move at different times even on the same line.

Reported `6X`, `7X`, and `FX` trains use their exact static route geometry when available, otherwise the existing `6`, `7`, or `F` geometry. A fallback moving segment requires both reported stops to be present; unsupported branches do not gain an invented path. The car retains its reported express ID and announces express service. ETA-driven motion still requires the exact reported trip, route, direction, next stop and ETA, so a base-route prediction cannot animate an express report. Unknown route suffixes and separate shuttles are not merged. This geometry fallback does not establish an express stopping pattern or change trip-planning boarding choices.

## Repeatable Simulator motion QA

For a Debug Simulator build, launch with both `--ui-test-fixture` and `--ui-test-temporal-motion`. This opt-in fixture keeps a fixed 120-second journey/arrival anchor, advances the reported Route 1 progress at the normal eight-second refresh cadence, then reports the train stopped at its next platform. A stationary Route 4 marker provides a camera reference. Ordinary UI fixtures, the production motion engine, polling, and physical-device builds are unchanged.

Add `--ui-test-temporal-motion-stale` to freeze the source generation, vehicle timestamp, and reported progress. Repeated requests cannot renew that evidence; the normal 60-second snapshot deadline still removes markers. Relaunching starts a separate journey. The existing default fixture repeats the same progress with a rolling ETA and can produce a legitimate anti-backjump hold, so it is unsuitable for sustained-motion judgments.

The focused UI test zooms the map, checks Route 1 displacement relative to the stationary marker, confirms the app entered the background, then checks movement again after reentry. This establishes rendered movement and resumption, not frame pacing or physical Duo smoothness. For visual comparison, record a stationary map with the Simulator's actual Reduce Motion preference off and on; compare within a feed generation as well as across reported updates. Restore the preference and stop the map after observation. A second rendered test confirms frozen source evidence removes both markers despite repeated requests. Model tests also cover stale expiry, stopped poses, invalid times, and discrete reduced-motion sampling. Record actual observations and limitations in the PR rather than interpreting a passing model test as an animation-quality result.

## Held station-update fixture

The three explicit flags `--ui-test-fixture --ui-test-station-motion --ui-test-motion-clock` enable a held clock only in **Debug Simulator** builds. The fixture control steps logical time through 0, 8, 16, 17, 18.5 and 20 seconds. The first new generation establishes the entry anchor; the next reports the adjacent station. Source generation and vehicle time then remain at 16 seconds while the real motion sampler advances through the four-second transition. Normal polling can repeat that source but cannot renew its evidence. Each step is blocked until the matching feed and sampled markers have been published; UI assertions separately wait for actual native-map displacement. This avoids racing sequential accessibility queries against a four-second wall-clock window.

The fixture uses the normal decoder, accepted-feed revision, motion model, map annotation, presentation wording and freshness checks. It changes neither the production duration nor assertion deadlines. Ordinary Simulator sessions, physical-device builds and Release builds have no clock/control override. Held steps demonstrate positions and accessibility presentation, not display pacing, FPS, CPU, energy use or physical fold behavior. The older failed wall-clock test and its unchanged passing repeat remain retained separately; they are not relabeled as a clean combined pass.

The narrow station-update case also supplies `--ui-test-motion-close-zoom`, selecting a representative close camera only inside that explicit Debug Simulator fixture. This inspects the maximum car scale with the actual native map. Expanded and largest-text cases retain the ordinary camera. The caption clears the full rotated artwork bounds, rather than the nominal unrotated marker height, while its overlay leaves the map anchor unchanged.
